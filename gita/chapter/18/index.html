Bhagavad Gita / Chapter 18

मोक्षसंन्यासयोगः

Mokṣa Sannyāsa Yoga — The Yoga of Liberation by Renunciation

The Gita's final chapter opens with Arjuna asking Krishna to finally distinguish sannyasa (renunciation) from tyaga (relinquishment), and Krishna answers by systematically re-running the guna-typology of chapters 14 and 17 across renunciation, action, knowledge, the actor, intellect, resolve, happiness, and finally the four varnas' duties — arguing that perfection comes through wholehearted engagement in one's own duty (svadharma) regardless of its content, echoing 3.35 almost verbatim. It then traces the path from purified reason through meditation to Brahman-attainment, which resolves into supreme bhakti rather than static abstraction, building to Krishna's climactic personal appeal and the Gita's single most consequential command: total surrender in exchange for liberation from all sin. The chapter — and the entire text — closes by resolving its own frame narrative: Arjuna's doubt-free declaration to act, directly reversing his chapter-1 collapse, and Sanjaya's closing testimony that wherever Krishna and Arjuna stand together, victory is assured.
