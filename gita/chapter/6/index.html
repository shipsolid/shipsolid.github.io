Bhagavad Gita / Chapter 6

अध्यात्मयोग

Adhyātma Yoga — The Yoga of Self-Subdual

Krishna gives Arjuna a detailed manual for meditation practice — a clean quiet seat, controlled posture, gaze fixed at the nose-tip, moderation in eating and sleep — building toward the mind's mastery over itself and the vision of the Self abiding equally in all beings. When Arjuna objects that the restless mind makes this goal unreachable, Krishna concedes the difficulty but insists it is attainable through practice (abhyasa) and dispassion (vairagya); when Arjuna then asks what becomes of one who strives sincerely but dies before perfecting the discipline, Krishna assures him that no effort toward the good is ever lost, carrying forward across rebirths until the goal is reached.

He that performeth such action as his duty, independently of the fruit of action, he is an ascetic, he is a Yogi, not he that is without fire, and without rites.

When a man feeleth no attachment either for the objects of sense or for actions, renouncing the formative will, then, he is said to be enthroned in yoga.

The higher Self of him who is Self-controlled and peaceful is uniform in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, as well as in honour and dishonour.

The Yogi who is satisfied with wisdom and knowledge, unwavering, whose senses are subdued, to whom a lump of earth, a stone and gold are the same, is said to be harmonised.

There, having made the mind one-pointed, with thought and the functions of the senses subdued, steady on his seat, he should practise yoga for the purification of the self.
